THE BEGINNING

What exactly
is tea?

Tea begins with the leaves of Camellia sinensis, an evergreen plant.

Black, green, oolong, and white tea come from this plant. Differences in the way the leaves are handled and processed help create their distinct character.

The tea in your cup is the result of both cultivation and craft — from the growing leaf to the decisions made after harvest.

ONE PLANT, DIFFERENT EXPRESSIONS

Meet the
tea family.

01

Black tea

Oxidation develops a darker leaf and the familiar rich character of black tea.

02

Green tea

Heating the leaves early limits oxidation and helps retain a greener character.

03

Oolong tea

Partially oxidised tea, with character shaped by how the leaves are processed.

04

White tea

Lightly processed tea made through careful withering and drying of selected leaf or buds.

FROM FIELD TO CUP

The making of
black tea.

A closer look at the main stages
of orthodox black tea manufacture.

01

Plucking

Young shoots are collected from the tea bush. Leaf selection is the beginning of the manufacturing journey.

02

Withering

Fresh leaves lose moisture and become softer, preparing them for rolling.

03

Rolling

The leaves are twisted and their cells disrupted, helping release the compounds involved in oxidation.

04

Oxidation

Contact with oxygen changes the leaf’s colour and develops the character of black tea.

05

Drying

Heat reduces moisture and brings oxidation to a stop.

06

Grading

The dried tea is sorted by particle size before packing. A grade describes leaf size rather than a simple quality score.
